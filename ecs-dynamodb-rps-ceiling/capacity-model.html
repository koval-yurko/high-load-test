Where the money goes

Share of provisioned cost by endpoint, at the frozen mix. Bars are ranked, not colour-coded — the label carries the identity.

■ Read capacity ■ Write capacity

Bytes returned per request is the cost driver — not reads versus writes. The feed endpoint is 25% of traffic and 61% of all read capacity, because a Query is billed on total bytes returned: 20×1 KB rounds to 5 RCU, five times a single GetItem.

An earlier flat read/write model made writes look like 71% of the bill. Adding one Query endpoint moved it to almost exactly even, so that finding was an artifact of the workload, not a property of DynamoDB.

The formula

From DynamoDB's capacity definitions, not from a price sheet.

Reading this before you pick

Cost is not the reason to choose provisioned mode. Provisioned is 3.46× cheaper than on-demand only at full utilisation. Break-even is about 29% — and a lab environment that idles between runs sits near that line. At realistic session utilisation the two modes cost roughly the same.

The reason to provision is the hard ceiling: overload becomes a throttle you can see on a dashboard, not an invoice you find later.

The real cost risk is a forgotten environment, not a load test. A 3-hour session at 1000 RPS of capacity costs about 96 cents. The same capacity left running for a month costs $234. The budget guard protects against the teardown you skipped, not the test you ran.

Throttling shows up as latency before it shows up as errors. The SDK retries ProvisionedThroughputExceededException with backoff, so an under-provisioned table inflates p95 first and only raises the error rate once retries are exhausted. Any p95 breach has to be checked against the dashboard's throttle-events panel — ReadThrottleEvents and WriteThrottleEvents, plotted as two lines — before it gets blamed on the service.

The first 25 RCU and 25 WCU are free each month. Reads bind first at this mix, so anything up to about 24 RPS of sustained capacity costs nothing at all — useful for smoke tests, useless as a ceiling worth measuring.
